Abstract: The rising prevalence of mental health challenges among university students has necessitated the development of systematic and technology-driven wellness monitoring mechanisms. Traditional institutional support systems are inherently reactive, triggered only when students self-identify as distressed or exhibit visible behavioral deterioration. Digital wellness platforms have emerged but face critical limitations: single-metric self-reporting vulnerability, data sparsity from forgotten check-ins, and oscillation artifacts from transient mood fluctuations. This paper presents AURA (An Adaptive AI System for Emotional and Academic Growth), a comprehensive institutional AI-driven student mental wellness and academic support platform. AURA introduces a multi-signal behavioral stress estimation framework that fuses six heterogeneous signals—self-reported mood, conversational sentiment, engagement activity, emotional volatility, temporal context, and trend momentum—through adaptive weight redistribution and exponential moving average (EMA) stabilization. The framework incorporates burst-detection anti-manipulation defense, z-score anomaly detection, and a three-factor confidence scoring mechanism. Beyond stress monitoring, AURA integrates AI-powered mental health chatbot support (Google Gemini 2.5 Flash), multimodal study assistance, grievance management, parental involvement portals, and emotion-aware UI personalization. Systematic evaluation across 10 synthetic behavioral archetypes demonstrates 92% variance reduction, 22% manipulation suppression, and monotonically calibrated confidence scoring. The platform is production-ready, deployed on Render with MongoDB Atlas, actively serving Aditya College of Engineering and Technology.

Keywords: Multi-signal stress monitoring, Adaptive behavioral fusion, Student mental wellness, AI-powered chatbot, Exponential moving average, Confidence scoring, Emotion-aware UI, Institutional wellness platform, Real-time analytics dashboard.

I. Introduction

Mental health challenges among university students have reached epidemic proportions, with epidemiological studies reporting 60–80% of undergraduates experiencing significant psychological stress during academic terms [1]. Traditional institutional response mechanisms—counselors, academic proctors, and peer support networks—are inherently reactive, triggered only when students self-identify as distressed or exhibit visible behavioral deterioration. This creates a critical detection gap: by the time institutional resources are mobilized, students are often in advanced crisis states.

Digital wellness platforms have emerged to address this gap, yet they present fundamental architectural limitations. The standard practice involves students selecting moods from predefined scales—an approach trivially gameable, inherently sparse when check-ins are forgotten, and susceptible to oscillation noise where single adverse entries immediately trigger false-positive alerts. Coaching and counseling services provide structure but lack real-time analytics, personalization, and financial accessibility.

AURA (An Adaptive AI System for Emotional and Academic Growth) addresses these constraints through a centralized, multi-signal behavioral architecture. The system combines six heterogeneous data sources—mood, sentiment, activity, volatility, temporal context, and trend—through adaptive weight redistribution and exponential moving average stabilization. This approach prevents phantom neutral anchoring during data sparsity while suppressing manipulation attempts through burst-detection algorithms.

The design philosophy emphasizes explainability over black-box machine learning. Every stress score computation follows deterministic, mathematically transparent rules suitable for institutional contexts where algorithmic accountability is paramount. Beyond passive monitoring, AURA provides active interventions: AI-powered mental health chatbot, multimodal study assistance, grievance workflows, and parental engagement portals—forming a complete institutional wellness ecosystem.

II. Literature Survey

Currently used student wellness monitoring systems are categorized as self-report instruments, passive sensing platforms, and institutional counseling models. Self-report tools (PHQ-9, GAD-7, Perceived Stress Scale) provide clinical validity but suffer from recall bias and completion fatigue. Passive sensing approaches, pioneered by Wang et al. [5] in the StudentLife project, demonstrate that smartphone behavioral proxies (call logs, accelerometer data, WiFi connectivity) can infer mental health trajectories without explicit student action.

Gjoreski et al. [3] established that multi-modal sensor fusion (heart rate variability, skin conductance, temperature) combined with contextual metadata yields significantly improved stress detection compared to single-modality approaches. Guntuku et al. [4] documented that linguistic markers in written communication correlate strongly with clinical depression scores, establishing text sentiment as a viable behavioral proxy.

Research in e-learning systems indicates that gamification and organized sequencing promote retention and engagement [10]. Mastery-based progression is more advantageous for long-term concept retention than passive content consumption [11]. However, existing platforms do not combine refined behavioral signals, automated validation, competitive ranking, and comprehensive analytics in a unified mental wellness ecosystem.

None of the current systems provide multi-signal behavioral fusion with adaptive weighting, burst-detection anti-manipulation, confidence-scored estimation, and integrated AI intervention within a mobile-first institutional architecture. AURA seals this gap by presenting an integrated ecosystem focusing on systematic advancement and quantifiable improvement in student mental wellness.

III. Existing & Proposed System

A. Existing System

Conventional wellness monitoring approaches are fragmented and reactive. Students depend on multiple independent platforms—mood tracking apps, counseling portals, academic systems—with no unified data flow. Progress tracking is irregular, and intervention mechanisms are not integrated into centralized dashboards. The lack of real-time analytics means deterioration patterns go undetected until crisis manifestation.

Single-signal self-reporting suffers from three critical vulnerabilities: (1) manipulation—students can report false positive moods to avoid institutional concern; (2) sparsity—forgotten check-ins produce no signal despite escalating stress; (3) oscillation—transient bad moods trigger immediate alert escalation, producing false positives.

B. Proposed System

AURA presents a centralized platform with organized modules for mental wellness monitoring, AI chatbot support, academic assistance, and institutional oversight. The core innovation is a six-signal behavioral fusion engine that computes stress scores from mood, sentiment, activity, volatility, temporal context, and trend signals.

Users interact through mood check-ins and AI chatbot conversations. The system extracts behavioral signals, applies adaptive weight redistribution based on data availability, smooths via EMA, and compresses through logistic function. A real-time dashboard displays stress scores with confidence badges, 7-day trends, and dominant factor explanations.

The proctor dashboard provides student watchlists with risk indicators. The HOD dashboard shows department-wide analytics. The parent portal enables OTP-authenticated access to child wellness data. The system guarantees organized monitoring, real-time tracking, and proactive intervention.

IV. Methodology

AURA is architected as a four-tier system comprising Flutter-based frontend (mobile/web), Flask backend (Python 3.12), MongoDB Atlas database, and Google Gemini AI integration. The workflow begins with user authentication and role assignment (student, proctor, HOD, parent). Behavioral signals are extracted from mood logs, chat conversations, and activity patterns.

D. Mathematical Foundation: Three-Stage Pipeline

Stage 1 — Signal Fusion:

Ŝt = Σi=1n w*i · xi (1)

where xi ∈ [0, 100] is the normalized signal value, w*i is the adaptive weight after redistribution, and n = 6.

Stage 2 — EMA Smoothing:

Stema = α · St-1 + (1 − α) · Ŝt (2)

where α = 0.6 (normal mode) or α = 0.3 (sparse-data mode).

Stage 3 — Logistic Compression:

St = 100 / (1 + e−k(Stema − μ)) (3)

where k = 0.08 (steepness) and μ = 50 (inflection point).

E. Adaptive Weight Redistribution Algorithm

When signal i lacks sufficient data (di = 0), its base weight is redistributed proportionally to signals with available data, preventing phantom neutral anchoring:

w*i = wi + [wi / Σj:d_j=1 wj] · Σk:d_k=0 wk (4)

This guarantees Σw*i = 1 always, preventing default-50 bias when signals are missing.

F. Anti-Manipulation Defense: Burst Detection

Threat Model: Student submits k ≥ 4 messages with uniform extreme sentiment within 300 seconds to artificially manipulate the sentiment score.

Defense: When burst is detected, the i-th most recent message receives dampened weight:

wchat,i = e−0.15i · √((i+1)/4) (5)

Resulting multipliers: {0.50, 0.71, 0.87, 1.00}. Newest (spammy) messages are suppressed most aggressively.

G. Confidence Scoring Framework

Confidence quantifies estimation reliability across three statistically independent factors:

C = (|D|/n · 0.4) + (min(r,10)/10 · 0.35) + max(0.05, 0.25 − σx/30 · 0.20) (6)

where |D|/n = data availability (40%), r/10 = sample size capped at 10 (35%), and σx = inter-signal variance (25%).

K. Algorithm: Stress Calculation Procedure

Procedure StressCalculation(user)

  1. Extract signals: mood, sentiment, activity, volatility, time_bias, trend
  2. Check data availability for each signal (di ∈ {0,1})
  3. Apply adaptive weight redistribution (Eq. 4)
  4. Compute weighted sum: Ŝt = Σ w*i · xi
  5. Apply EMA smoothing: Stema = α · St-1 + (1−α) · Ŝt
  6. Apply logistic compression: St = 100/(1 + e−0.08(S−50))
  7. Compute confidence score C (Eq. 6)
  8. If (score > 75 AND trend = 'up' AND volatility > 55) OR score > 90: Trigger alert
  9. Persist to MongoDB with full audit trail
  10. Return {score, confidence, dominant_factor, explanation}

End Procedure

VII. Future Scope

The proposed AURA platform establishes a well-organized, analytics-based foundation for student mental wellness monitoring. Several enhancements can improve scalability, flexibility, and intelligence:

AI-Based Personalization: Through performance evaluation in mood patterns, chat engagement, and response latency, adaptive algorithms can dynamically adjust intervention recommendations based on individual learner characteristics.

Machine Learning Predictive Analytics: Based on historical performance data, the system can predict probability of crisis escalation, enabling proactive intervention scheduling before deterioration manifests.

Natural Language Processing Enhancement: Integration of contextual language models (beyond keyword matching) would improve sentiment detection accuracy for nuanced emotional expressions.

Voice-Based Interaction: Google Speech-to-Text integration would enable hands-free chatbot interaction, improving accessibility and reducing barriers to engagement.

Peer Support Forums: Anonymous, moderated discussion boards where students share experiences would build community-driven mental health support.

Counselor Appointment Scheduling: Calendar integration with institutional psychology services would enable seamless transition from automated support to human intervention.

Cross-Platform Support: Web dashboards alongside mobile applications would enhance accessibility across device types.

Gamification Extensions: Achievement badges, streak rewards, and wellness challenges would increase engagement motivation.

VIII. Conclusion

This paper presents AURA, a production-grade institutional AI platform for student mental wellness monitoring, demonstrating that multi-signal behavioral approaches provide measurably superior stress estimation compared to single-metric self-reporting. Through adaptive weight redistribution, AURA gracefully handles data sparsity without phantom neutral anchoring. EMA + logistic compression achieves 92% total variance reduction while maintaining crisis sensitivity (detecting high-risk thresholds within 5 sensor readings).

Burst-detection anti-manipulation mechanisms suppress sentiment gaming by 22% while maintaining cross-signal robustness through appropriate mood-signal dominance. The three-factor confidence score provides reliable uncertainty quantification (monotonic, 0.62-unit span) enabling meaningful UI communication about estimation reliability.

Beyond core monitoring, AURA integrates AI-powered mental health chatbot (Gemini 2.5 Flash), multimodal study assistant, grievance management, and parental portals—forming a comprehensive institutional wellness ecosystem. The architecture is intentionally transparent and rule-based, avoiding black-box machine learning to remain defensible under academic scrutiny.

The complete platform is production-ready, deployed on Render + MongoDB Atlas, actively serving Aditya College of Engineering and Technology. Through integrated behavioral fusion, automated validation, and real-time analytics, AURA creates a student-centric, scalable ecosystem for modern institutional mental wellness support.
